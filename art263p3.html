How are we remembered?

Every day we walk (or drive) past a story.


Just before sunrise on January 21st 2019, Kenneth Leys’ vehicle plunged into the frigid waters of the Willamette. Highway 20 curves, but he drove straight.

Almost seven years later, the scene shows no trace of his end, nature has wiped the slate clean. The fragility and impermanence of our lives is illuminated by banked white and yellow lines - lines that we can’t always see.

We’ve grown to live on this precipice of accepted risks, some physical, some metaphorical. We live parts of our life mere moments from an end, and don’t give it a second thought. Should we leave, what shape will our memory take?

In Kenneth’s case, he lives on through the river, invisibly flowing and ebbing.

I loved the idea of the past surrounding us. In this project, I focused on a specific story - a car crash from years prior. I love the reflection of it, in that only a few years have passed, but the traces are almost gone. Allowing the landscape to tell the story was my primary goal.
